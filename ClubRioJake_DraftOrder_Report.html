Does draft order matter in Club Rio Jake?

15 seasons · 2011–2025 · 180 team-seasons · Yahoo league #183003

Draft slot vs regular-season win %

r = −0.02

No. Across 15 seasons the pick you drew explains essentially none of how your season went — not your record, not your points, not your playoff run. Draft slot accounts for 0.04% of the variance in win percentage. The strongest signal anywhere in the data is a mild points-scored edge for early picks (r = −0.10, p = 0.18) — still not statistically significant.

Method and caveats

  • Where the numbers come from. Read directly off the league's own Yahoo pages for each of the 15 seasons: the Standings tab (record, points for, final rank), the Draft Results page (round 1 order = draft slot), and the Managers page (team-to-manager mapping). Retrieved 13 August 2026.
  • Team names change; managers don't. Managers rename teams constantly, so franchises are tracked by manager. Cory alone has been Big Red Machine, Mean Machine, Fournetflix n Chill, Dark Shadows, Fighting Mickeys and Broken Rhules. One caveat: the name PappyVanDrinkle was Keith's in 2023 and Jace's in 2024 — Yahoo carried the name to a new manager, and they are treated as separate franchises here.
  • Points scored are not directly comparable across eras. Season length went from 13 games to 14 in 2021, and scoring settings have been tweaked over the years. That's why every points comparison in this report is per game.
  • Sign conventions. For win percentage and points, a negative correlation means earlier picks did better. For final finish and points-scored rank — where 1 is best — a positive correlation means earlier picks did better.
  • Sample size is the real limit. Fifteen observations per draft slot. With n = 180 pooled, a correlation needs |r| > 0.15 to clear p < 0.05; within a single season (n = 12) it needs |r| > 0.58. Slot-level records — slot 8's five titles, slot 7's two playoff berths — are fun, not findings.
  • What this does not test. Draft slot is not the same as draft quality. A league where everyone drafts about equally well would show exactly this result even if the first pick were genuinely valuable, because good managers at slot 12 offset it. This measures whether the slot alone predicted the outcome — it didn't.
  • One data oddity worth flagging. Yahoo's stored round 1 for the 2014 and 2015 drafts is almost entirely quarterbacks, which no normal draft produces. The team order in those rounds is used as-is; if those two seasons' draft slots look wrong to you, they're worth a manual check. Excluding them does not change any conclusion here.
